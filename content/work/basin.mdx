## The problem

<Prompt>Open on the stakes. Your words from the interview, to edit: "It was the difference between a morning where the first thing I do when I wake up is touch my face to feel for acne before I even get out of bed, and rolling over to my husband without shame. It's the difference between having to be caked in makeup or choosing if i want to wear it or not. It's the difference between feeling beautiful or not."</Prompt>

<Prompt>Then the years. Your words: "I have struggled with my skin my whole life, and my desperation to fix it resulted in years of frantically adding and removing products from my routine in a desperate hope that something would eventually kick into place and calm it all down. I learned the hard way, after a rosacea type 2 diagnoses and outbreaks so bad that doctors thought it was staph, that the only way to do it was to strip it all the way down to basics and then slowly add things in. Doing this and slowly tracking my progress with careful observations was the only way that I was able to get my skin not only to a place of peace, but to a place of confidence." Decide whether the diagnosis stays on a public page.</Prompt>

<Prompt>The method and where it broke. Your words: "I slowly added something and watched my face change, but it was hard to compare and oberve if things were really improving or not. So instead, I started taking pictures every day. But there was no way for me to carefully compare, and keeping track of the pictures dates, what I was testing, what I thought was laborsome"</Prompt>

<Prompt>The thought at the mirror. Your words: "One day I was taking a picture and thought how nice it would be to line up my face with past pictures and slide it to compare. That started the whole idea"</Prompt>
