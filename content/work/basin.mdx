Standing in front of the mirror each morning is a gamble when you have bad skin. For most of my life, before I even get out of bed, I wonder: "did that product I put on last night work?"

Desperate for clear skin and a clear head, I bought products and tested them like a machine. My conclusions were often guesses. It wasn't until I stripped my skincare routine back to basics and slowly integrated new products, that I discovered what worked.

Basin is the witness to the expedition for clear skin.

## Decisions

{/* DRAFT (Claude, Nov 3) from Emily's own logged lines in content/decisions/;
    she edits. The point of the section: the model generated options at every
    fork, and the calls below are hers, several of them against the model. */}

Basin was built with an AI in the room from the first night. It generated options at every fork and argued for most of them. These are the five calls I made, and what I was looking at when I made them.

**One timeline, not one per trial.** The model proposed parallel trials, each with its own photo series. I kept one timeline: a photo belongs to a date, and a trial is whatever span of dates carried that tag, derived when you look. There is no trials table, on purpose. A second record of "what am I testing" is a second thing that can disagree with the photos.

**The photo is the hinge.** You cannot start a trial without taking a photo; the only function in the app that creates a trial takes an image. A trial that exists before its day-one photo has nowhere to write itself. Starting one on a past day attaches it to that day's photo and carries it forward.

**The user is the only clock, and then a check-in.** The model wanted expectation windows per product type, check-ins at weeks 2, 4, 8 and 12, a countdown. None of it shipped. Basin does not decide when someone is allowed to look at their own face. Then I used it for a month and watched my own trials sit open with nobody deciding. So one thing came back, in the form the rule allows: a four-week check-in that asks "Still testing?", where "still testing" is a complete answer. Four weeks is one skin-cell turnover, the floor for judging almost anything.

**Worked / Didn't work.** The first verdicts were Kept and Stopped: facts about what you did, because "worked" is a claim the photos can't prove. I changed them. People say things worked or didn't work for them all the time; it's a person's verdict about their own face, in the words they'd use. No colour carries the verdict, either.

**On this phone, and nowhere else.** No account, no server, nothing uploaded. Export everything as a zip with a letter; hold to erase, with no dialog. The About page is a heading, one sentence and two buttons, because privacy is a feature here, not a policy page.
