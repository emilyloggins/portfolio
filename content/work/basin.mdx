Standing in front of the mirror each morning is a gamble when you have bad skin. For most of my life, before I even get out of bed, I wonder: "did that product I put on last night work?"

Desperate for clear skin and a clear head, I bought products and tested them like a machine. My conclusions were often guesses. It wasn't until I stripped my skincare routine back to basics and slowly integrated new products, that I discovered what worked.

Basin is the witness to the expedition for clear skin.

## Decisions

This project taught me how to use discernment in an AI forward workflow. These are some human decisions that were made along the way.

**Never let AI pick up the pen.** I think letting AI take the lead on microcopy makes a product feel like no one made it.

**One photo, one timeline.** The model suggested running parallel trials with their own photos, but I knew from the start that I wanted a single photo to be the cornerstone. Like coming to the sink basin every day and looking in the mirror to check your skin. We have enough going on, let's only do it once.

**The user goes at their own pace.** The model also wanted to implement check-ins and streaks to keep the user engaged daily. None of that shipped. As the heart of this product is compassion, I rejected anything that could be guilt inducing. I implemented a monthly check-in for trials that don't have a verdict yet, which can be dismissed permanently by the user upon first in-app notification. The user can test as long as they like.

**On this phone and nowhere else.** Nothing is uploaded, intentionally. I wanted privacy to be a feature, and the experience to feel unbridled by an account.
